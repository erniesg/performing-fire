MICROSITE / VISUAL LANGUAGE STUDY

THE BROADCAST NEEDS A BODY.

Performing Fire gathers separate offerings into one transmission. The microsite asks what kind of structure can hold them without flattening their differences.

01 / OBJECT → SYSTEM

FROM SCREEN TO SYSTEM

Nam June Paik treated television as object, instrument, and building block. We began there: not by quoting one work, but by asking how one screen could become an architecture.

CANDLE TV held one signal. SLABS repeated it. RACK B and the OPEN FRAME exposed the support structure. The direction settled into a modular wall: distinct channels, one apparatus.

02 / BEHAVIOUR + RECEPTION

A SIGNAL DOES MORE THAN APPEAR

It falls, listens, carries, and returns. Water gave the system behaviour—fall, collision, break. The satellite dish and Cheomseongdae turned reception into visible form. DATA JANGSEUNG stood at the threshold between human offering and machine transmission.

03 / TOTEM → VESSEL

FROM TOTEM TO VESSEL

The form studies moved through totems, stupas, moon jars, and the temporary reference of a cyberpunk columbarium. Each tested how fragments might gather into one presence.

Moon jars offered a quieter model: two halves making one vessel. The stupa studies tested vertical assembly—ceramic, wind, jewel, celadon—before the microsite returned to its simplest unit: a container for a signal.

SETTLED DIRECTION

FIVE CONTAINERS. ONE BROADCAST.

The final console is not a sculpture rendered on screen. It is a system of containers. ABOUT sets the premise. CONTRIBUTE receives voices. EXPERIMENTS tests materials. RESEARCH holds the corpus. LOG keeps time. Each preview carries a different signal; together they make the Broadcast.
